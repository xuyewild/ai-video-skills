# Action Writing Rules for Video Models (Seedance 2.5 as the Example)

A summary of web sources and the author's production tests. The numbers match [sources.md](sources.md). Items marked "judgment" have no direct source and were written from body mechanics or experience. For hard limits of other video models (length, number of reference images), follow their own official documents. The writing principles apply across models.

## 1. Writing rules (2.5)

| # | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write setup → contact → result in full, with the result last. Do not give only the start and the ending (given only the two ends, the model invents the transition) | S9, S10, S2 |
| 2 | One main action per time segment. Too much content in a segment causes over-cutting or dropped beats. When events are dense, reduce the stages instead of cutting finer. Fights are the exception: one shot may chain 2-3 attacker and defender displacements (first ... then right away ... then ...). Effect chains work the same way. A non-dialogue everyday shot does one thing and then cuts. A dialogue shot still has one main action | S1, S6, S11, S2. The fight exception comes from third-party samples |
| 3 | No upper limit on the number of beats. The number of beats is set by the action and the camera needs. S7 and S6 only give observations of beat density, which are not converted into a formula | S7, S6 |
| 4 | The length of one beat (setup-contact-result) is set by what the action needs, at the shortest about 0.25 seconds. Timestamps may go down to 0.25 seconds. Fight cuts have no lower limit on shot length | Judgment (the 1.25-second average in S8's fast-cut example measures time between cuts, not the length of a beat) |
| 5 | Write direction, distance, speed, contact, and reaction for important actions. Background action does not need to be written | S7 |
| 6 | Strikes need weight: recoil, follow-through, the struck side pushed away. Do not let it "float" | S8, S4 |
| 7 | Fast actions blur and deform. Allow directional blur only on the fastest instant, and write "face, hands, and weapon are sharp again as soon as the action ends". Use simple moves in place of a "spinning, jumping, chained kick" | S12, S13. For simple moves see S19 (2.0) |
| 8 | Multi-person contact and complex action physics are weak points the vendor admits. One main pair of contact per beat. Before contact, write the distance and which hand each uses | S3 |
| 9 | Write the weapon into the subject. Lock one kind of attack (only chop or only thrust), edge first. The constraint reads "the weapon does not float" | S8, S10, S19 |
| 10 | Slow motion only for the deciding beat (about 2 seconds). Mark entry and exit, and write that everyone else "stays at normal speed". Freeze frame, bullet time, and a full-screen white flash are allowed at most once each per video | S8, S4, S14 |
| 11 | The camera move is triggered by the action. One shot can combine moves, written in order. In action scenes a whip pan can lead into the next exchange | S1, S10, S4 |
| 12 | Do not write all actions as "simultaneous". Write "at the same time" only when it is truly simultaneous | S10 |
| 13 | When continuing or extending, write the movement already in progress, and do not write the last beat again | S10, S16 |
| 14 | In a 30-second long segment, every part needs a new change of state (new contact, change of direction, change of height, someone falls), or the second half is monotonous | S15 |
| 15 | In a group battle, space out the background characters and do not allow cloned people. Each person appears only once in the frame. 1-8 subjects is the most stable | S4, S8, S6 |
| 16 | Replace result words with process: change "fiercely", "suddenly", and "tremendous impact force" into body part, direction, displacement, landing point | S5 |
| 17 | The struck side is the subject in the hit shot. Writing only "hit" or "light" gets a body that does not move. For the overall displacement of a large static structure, write it as a separate item, and if it still does not move, change it to a breakup | S18 |
| 18 | State whether it is attack or defense. Writing only "does not dodge or block" gets a shield added | S18 |
| 19 | Anime-intensified style: impact frames and speed lines can be written, with no more than two style anchors. The art style follows the visual bible. A 3D project is not written as pure 2D cel style, and a 2D project does not get volumetric light and realistic materials | S17, S4. The second half is judgment |
| 20 | People with animals: write the relative height as numbers and attach a scale reference image. Write the contact part down to a specific structure | Judgment (no dedicated source found) |
| 21 | Write results down to the marks and the way they stop: how many steps the struck side slides back, what is plowed under the feet, what steadies them | S21. Consistent with rules 6 and 17 |
| 22 | Strength comes from the difference in displacement (one side backs off five steps, the other three) and two reactions to the same hit (one kneels, one does not move), not from adjectives | S21 |
| 23 | Impact has three levels: light, heavy, finisher. Each level has a fixed body, environment, and camera feedback. Most of a video is light and heavy | S21 |
| 24 | Camera shake only at the instant of contact, along the force direction, then returns level at once, and leave beats without shake. No shaking throughout | S21 |
| 25 | Write a purpose for each attack: block the path, force the opponent to change direction, interrupt a charge-up | S21 |
| 26 | Write action by direction, distance, speed, contact, reaction. Use specific move names ("right straight punch", "double-forearm block"), and do not write "swift" or "epic" | S22, S23 |
| 27 | The first shot is a wide shot establishing positions (fight exception: if they are already fighting at second 0, write the left and right positions into the character relationship part, and re-establish once in the middle with a top-down or wide shot). One person leads at any moment. When characters tend to stick together, widen the initial gap | S23, S25 |
| 28 | For speed changes, state at which moment slow motion starts and when it ends. The switch point is not always exact, and the slow-motion word is written only once | S24 |
| 29 | Write the "hold for a beat" at the instant of contact as a visible state ("the two of them, together with the sparks, hold for a beat"), not as a frame count. The whole clip may freeze, to be verified | Judgment. The freeze risk is from S24 |
| 30 | A charge or a move does not stop before contact: neither the camera nor the video lands on "the fist is one step from the chest" or "pouncing in mid-air" and waits for something else. If you need to insert a line of dialogue or someone else's action, calculate the time by distance first. If it does not fit, move the charge's starting point farther back and put the dialogue before the charge starts, or crosscut it into the charge, with every cut back to the attacker closer, and the contact landing on the last line of dialogue | Author's test: the attacker rushed to one step away, stopped and struck a pose, and only threw the punch after about 10 seconds of dialogue |
| 31 | The hit shot shows the receiver: in the contact shot the camera is in front of or beside the struck side, and an over-the-shoulder shot goes only over the attacker's shoulder. The hit shot starts on the instant of contact (contact → force → displacement begins), not from "already in mid-air" | Author's test: the camera looked past the receiver's shoulder at the attacker, and in the finished clip the attacker punched air while the receiver did not move. The next shot started from "flying backward, one body-height off the ground", and the cause of the flight was never shot |
| 32 | Write the same beat only once and do not pin down positions: write setup, pulling back the fist, letting go, braking, and landing only once across adjacent shots and across clips, and write continuation as "is ...ing". Do not write the person who is hit as "always at one spot". Write "at the start at ..., after being hit stops at ...". The direction of the knockaway, the thing hit, and the scene positions must match | Author's test: the same fist pull-back was written twice. The steps the person was thrown into were actually on the attacker's side |

## 2. Known failures and countermeasures

| Symptom | Version | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| The arm is blocked by the torso and disappears | Cross-model reference S28 (Wan 3.0 / MiniMax, not Seedance) | Use a side angle for key contact, and write "whole body in frame, limbs not blocked by the torso" |
| The camera ducks away at the instant of contact | Cross-model reference S28 | Keep the camera on the contact point and write "the camera does not move away" |
| Robes, limbs, and blade edges go soft in fast sections | 2.5 test S12 | Rule 7. Fewer beats. Hold for a beat after the action |
| Limbs deform or extra limbs appear in fast sections | 2.5 test S13, S6 | Simple moves. Write "normal number of hands and feet, no deformation" |
| Fist or blade passes through the target (clipping) | 2.0 reference S19. Listed as something to avoid in 2.5 official examples S4 | Hit in its own shot. Write the contact part and the struck side's displacement. Write the distance between the two first |
| Weapon changes shape or disappears | 2.5 test S12. 2.0 reference S19 | Write the weapon into the subject. Lock the holding hand and the blade direction |
| The lead looks slow-motion while others are at normal speed | 2.5 test S14 | When slow motion is not needed, write "normal speed throughout". When needed, mark only one beat |
| The second half of a 30-second fight is monotonous | 2.5 test S15 | Rule 14 |
| An extended segment does not connect to the previous action | 2.5 test S16 | Write the movement direction and posture in progress into the opening frame |
| The struck side does not move and only a streak of light appears | 2.5 test S18 | Rules 17 and 31 |
| Direction reversed or front and back contradict | 2.5 test S18 | Write the force direction every beat and check it once across the whole passage |
| A shield is added automatically | 2.5 test S18 | Rule 18 |
| Duplicate or swapped people with multiple references | 2.5 official S6 | Write the purpose of each reference image line by line |

## 3. How action blocks go into the video prompt

- The three sentences "setup, contact, result" of an action block go into the description of the matching shot. The "force direction" goes into the sentence about character relationships or direction lock. "Camera" is a suggestion, and the camera is finally decided by the person writing the prompt.
- When one beat spans two shots (setup in one, the hit in another), write the "contact" in the hit shot, with the struck side as the subject.
- When an effect and an action happen in the same hit: write the action only down to "the contact point, the direction, the body's first reaction", write the effect process separately, and merge them into the same event.

## 3A. Debugging order for generation

First use a fixed wide angle to check that the choreography is right, then add camera moves. Change only one variable at a time. Rewrite only the beat that broke, and do not tear down the whole segment (S23). When something goes wrong, check first: whether the space conflicts, whether the contact is clearly written, how many competing tasks are crammed into one clip, whether dialogue takes up the action time, whether a reference image is bound wrongly.

## 4. Other differences in Seedance 2.0

| Item | 2.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 4-30 seconds | 4-15 seconds |
| Style | Closer to realism | More willing to make stylized deformation |
| Reference images | Many | Fewer. When a person and an animal share a frame, keep the scale reference image first |
| Prompt | - | The two versions' writing is not directly interchangeable |
