# Sources

Scope: Seedance 2.5 is the standard. 2.0 material is used only as reference and is marked. Only 2026 material is used. Looked up in 2026-10. Models change fast, so re-check before use.

Version labels: **2.5 official** = official documents or release notes from ByteDance, Volcengine, or Jimeng (Dreamina). **2.5 tutorial** = third-party guides that clearly target 2.5. **2.5 test** = tests with generated results. **2.0 reference** = material that targets 2.0.

## Could not be found

- **Spirit-beast and human grabs, throws, and lifts**: no dedicated source or test for Seedance 2.5 or 2.0 was found. The related rules are written from body mechanics and the scale baseline, and are judgments.
- **Chibi exaggerated action**: no dedicated Seedance source was found. The writing uses "mark the entry and exit" and writing proportions separately by time segment, and is a judgment.
- **Reversed force direction**: no dedicated test was found. There is only the author's record of directions written backward (S18).
- **Lost weapon handoffs**: no dedicated 2.5 source. There is only 2.0's "weapon disappears between frames" (S19) and the 2.5 tutorial's "lock one kind of attack" (S8).
- **Difference between group battles and one-on-one**: no direct comparison. It is inferred from the vendor-admitted weakness in multi-subject interaction (S3) and the stable range of subject counts (S4).
- **Upper limit of action beats in one video**: no official number. S7 and S6 give observations of beat density, which are not converted into a formula.

## Action-design craft sources (not Seedance, used for sections 11-13 of body-and-contact)

- Dyabin, "Separation / Compensation Principle in Strikes Animation", 80.lv, 2021-12-10, https://80.lv/articles/separation-compensation-principle-in-strikes-animation/ : the body stops first and the limb goes out after, and that is what gives weight.
- PlayStation Blog, "God of War Ragnarök" combat design, 2022-10-04, https://blog.playstation.com/?p=370399 : big changes in the hit posture, large-arc swings with strong follow-through, deliberately reducing screen shake for readability, sound making up the impact.
- Notes on Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer)'s game feel talk, 2013-10-22, https://infovore.org/?p=5275 : a short freeze on hit, knockback along the attack direction, the camera kicks along the attack direction and then decays.
- Masahiro Sakurai on hitstop, translated by Source Gaming, 2015-11-11, https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/ : both sides stop at the same time, the receiver shakes, and the duration grows with damage.
- No Film School, "Jackie Chan's 9 principles of action comedy", 2014-12-03, https://nofilmschool.com/2014/12/jackie-chans-9-principles-action-comedy : clear camera, action and reaction in the same frame, make people feel the rhythm, start from a disadvantage, pain gives the character humanity.
- RogerEbert.com interview with Gareth Evans and Iko Uwais, 2014-03-25, https://www.rogerebert.com/features/the-art-of-controlled-chaos-with-gareth-evans-and-iko-uwais-of-the-raid-2-berandal : each character has their own rhythm, and the receiver does not move early.
- HK01, "Realistic vs stylized: action design in martial-arts films", 2018 (updated 2025), https://www.hk01.com/%E6%AD%A6%E5%82%99%E5%BF%97/149832/ : the big open northern school and realistic fighting-style design.
- Xinhua interview with Yuen Woo-ping, 2026-02-19, https://www.news.cn/20260219/233171a833994189bfdc6aa4d20e003b/c.html : differences in weapons are differences in character.
- GCORES, "The Hit Feel of Action Games", 2018-05-22, https://www.gcores.com/articles/97115 : the higher the move tier, the stronger the feedback, with wind-up and recovery.
- The Paper's review of Fog Hill of Five Elements, 2020-08-10, https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_8657966 : fights have a rising, developing, turning, closing arc, and real weapon work is paired with stylized effects (the reporter's analysis).
